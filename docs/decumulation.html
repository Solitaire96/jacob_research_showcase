Deep-RL retirement decumulation

How much should a retiree spend each year, and how much should stay in stocks, when markets and lifespan are both uncertain? Theory has a closed-form answer for a stylised version. This asks whether a model-free agent finds it on its own, and whether it holds up on two decades it never saw.

The problem, in one paragraph

A 65-year-old has $1M and $2,000 a month of guaranteed income. Once a year until death they choose an equity weight and a withdrawal rate; returns, rebalancing and withdrawals run monthly. The objective is expected CRRA utility of real consumption (risk aversion γ = 4), weighted by Gompertz survival (median death age 85.7). Lifetimes are 12-month block bootstraps of real US returns: the agent trains on 1955–1999 only, and is scored on 2000–2026, which holds the dot-com crash, 2008, 2020 and the 2022 bond crash. The score is certainty-equivalent consumption: the constant monthly spending worth the same expected utility as the policy's uncertain stream. Two actor-critic algorithms are trained, DDPG (Lillicrap et al., 2016) and TD3 (Fujimoto et al., 2018), five seeds each.

The leak, caught and reproduced

Blocks of real history make lifetimes realistic, and they make memorisation easy. When the agent saw last month's Fed funds rate, term spread and inflation at each annual decision, that triplet identified the calendar month, and with it the next twelve months of returns. A 64×64 network learned the map. The tell is the gap between in-sample and held-out scores. The table gives each agent's score minus the tuned annuitized rule's, in real $/month.

The fix lags macro state along the simulated path, not the source history, so at a block boundary the agent sees the previous, independent block. At annual decisions that leaves nothing to learn from, and the default agent observes only wealth and age. The "leaky lag" rows reproduce the bug on purpose (train.py --leaky); the "+ macro" rows keep the macro features with the fix in place.

Honest limits

  • In this setting the closed-form rule is close to optimal, so the best an agent can do is roughly match it, and that is what happens: a small held-out edge that reverses with 60-month blocks. That is evidence the RL machinery works, not that RL beats theory. An edge would need genuine state dependence (a fitted regime or VAR return model, fat-tailed shocks, taxes, annuity purchases), none of which is in this simulator.
  • Returns are index returns: CRSP total market and a 10-year Treasury built from monthly-average yields. No fees, no taxes, no credit, no TIPS.
  • Mortality is a two-parameter Gompertz law (m = 88, b = 10, after Milevsky, 2012), not a life table; no couples, health or long-term-care shocks.
  • 2000–2026 is one realisation of history. Bootstrapping it gives many lifetimes, but they share its regimes; the in-sample tuned rules prefer 100% equity, which that window punished.
  • Nothing here is peer-reviewed. The 2026 rebuild was done with AI coding assistants under my direction; the repo's ledger records what was checked and how.
