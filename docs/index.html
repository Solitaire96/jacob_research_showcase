Flux-cone automata

Dynamic flux balance analysis assumes a microbe always picks its growth-maximising fluxes. Give dFBA an exact hybrid-automaton semantics and you can ask what the culture could do under any feasible strategy, and certify the answer.

The idea, in one paragraph

At every time step, dFBA (Mahadevan et al., 2002) solves a linear program for the organism's fluxes and advances the medium. The hybrid automaton has one location per conic generator of the organism's flux cone; dwell time in a location is integrated flux along that generator; biomass updates when a run completes. The main theorem: the automaton's states at step boundaries are exactly the states dFBA can reach when each step may use any feasible flux, not only the greedy one. Reachability over all strategies then becomes convex optimisation (LPs for yields and certificates, convex programs for viability), and on small models it is machine-checked in KeYmaera X and Z3.

Theorem (dynamic decomposition)

Let each dFBA step use any feasible flux. The automaton's states right after each closing transition, restricted to the medium and biomass, are exactly these dFBA trajectories.

Proved by hand in general (written proof in the manuscript in preparation, not in this repo). Machine-checked in KeYmaera X on the co-culture: one step in each direction (P4, P5) and trajectories of any length (P7, P8, P8g). Properties hold at step boundaries; states inside a step are not dFBA states.

What a co-culture looks like as an automaton

Two organisms share a medium. Organism 2 secretes C; organism 1 can take C up and turn it into Y, a metabolite neither makes alone. The construction takes only the stoichiometry, the flux bounds and the initial medium, computes the conic generators of each organism's flux cone exactly (checked, and at red-blood-cell scale computed, with cddlib's double description method; Fukuda & Prodon, 1996), and turns each generator into a location of a hybrid automaton.

Beyond the toy: a red blood cell and a genome-scale E. coli pair

Human red blood cell: doomed before it looks doomed

iAB-RBC-283 (Bordbar et al., 2011), as modified for uFBA (Bordbar et al., 2017); 216 metabolites × 271 reactions. Numbers from runs in my working folder; only the co-culture is reproduced in this repo.

A myopic dFBA policy can steer a cell into a state from which no strategy survives, hours before anything visible fails. The work gives an exact closed form for which states are viable and when doom arrives, and checks it against LP certificates on every grid point: viability on 272 of 272, doom time on 86 of 86. For the max-urate policy the doomed region is a band exactly 1 mM of glucose wide, with up to 8 hours of lead time.

All-strategy viability is a convex program, and conservation laws such as urate secreted ≤ adenine consumed are proved for every strategy in KeYmaera X (sample below).

Genome-scale E. coli consortium: dFBA can't decide, the envelope can

Zhang & Reed (2014) lysine/leucine auxotroph pair on iJO1366 (Orth et al., 2011), ~1,675 active reactions each. Growth rate, h−1. Numbers from runs in my working folder, not reproduced in this repo.

Per-step selfish dFBA, by tie-break Certified maximum, balanced growth Measured

Neither strain can grow alone (certified by LP). Per-step dFBA is ill-posed here: the answer depends on an arbitrary tie-break. The 0.98 h−1 maximum is a static LP certificate for balanced growth. The dynamic all-strategy envelope is a single LP of about 80,000 variables; it uses an Euler biomass step and approaches 0.98 as Δt → 0. One release cap, fitted to the measured rate, brings the community down to it; that is a fit, not a prediction.

A sample proof

The co-culture automaton, the red-blood-cell properties and the comparison theorem below are machine-checked in KeYmaera X (Fulton et al., 2015), a theorem prover for differential dynamic logic (Platzer, 2008). The first proof below is short enough to read: over every strategy the red-blood-cell automaton can follow, urate secreted never exceeds adenine consumed (comments added).

ArchiveEntry "RBC P1: urate secreted <= adenine consumed, all strategies"
ProgramVariables
  Real ade; Real x; Real ga; Real gx; Real Bio; Real A0; Real X0;
End.
Problem
  Bio >= 0 & ade = A0 & x = X0
  ->
  [{
     { {ga := -3; gx := 0;}          /* choose any generator branch ... */
       ++ {ga := -1; gx := 0;}
       ++ {ga := -1; gx := 1;}
       ++ {ga := 0;  gx := 0;} };
     { ade' = Bio*ga, x' = Bio*gx }  /* ... flow along it for any time ... */
   }*                                /* ... and repeat, any number of times */
   @invariant(x - X0 <= A0 - ade & Bio >= 0)
  ] x - X0 <= A0 - ade              /* urate made <= adenine used, always */
End.

The harder results are continuous-time comparisons. This one says that for one organism on one substrate under Michaelis–Menten uptake, no admissible strategy ever accumulates more biomass than the greedy one. The proof introduces the greedy trajectory as a differential ghost, polynomialises the rational dynamics with reciprocal ghosts, and closes the comparison with a Darboux inequality. KeYmaera X checks it in about a second.

ArchiveEntry "ThmC-ii mm: B <= b along every piecewise-constant strategy, cap Michaelis-Menten V*B*G/(K+G)"
Problem
  V>0 & K>0 & Y>0
  & B >= 0 & G >= 0 & B + Y*G = P0 & b = B
  & r*(K*Y + P0 - b) = 1 & rB*(-B + K*Y + P0) = 1      /* reciprocal ghosts */
  ->
  [{ u := *; g := *;                                    /* any uptake and growth ... */
     ?(0<=u & u*(G + K) <= B*(G*V) & 0<=g & g<=Y*u);   /* ... within the MM cap */
     {B' = g, G' = -u,
      b' = P0*V*Y*b*r - V*Y*b^2*r,                      /* greedy biomass, as a ghost */
      r' = P0*V*Y*b*r^3 - V*Y*b^2*r^3, rB' = g*rB^2
      & G>=0 & u*(G + K) <= B*(G*V)}
   }*
  ] (B <= b & ...)                                      /* never beats greedy */
End.
Tactic "dbx comparison (generated)"
  implyR(1); andL('L)*;
  loop("B >= 0 & G >= 0 & B + Y*G <= P0 & B <= b & ...", 1); <(
    QE, QE,
    unfold; dC("r*(K*Y + P0 - b) - 1 = 0", 1); <( ... dbx(...); ... ), dbx("-V*Y*b*r", 1) ), ...
  )
End.

Honest limits

  • Nothing here is peer-reviewed. A 2021 version received a revise-and-resubmit from IEEE Systems Journal; the revision and a conference version are in preparation.
  • The yield and conservation certificates are classical LP facts, applied here to all strategies. The continuous-time comparison under Michaelis–Menten, Hill and Haldane caps with cone-decomposed fluxes is, to my knowledge, new.
  • The repo reproduces the toy co-culture (dFBA replay, reachable band, controlled automaton) and holds the KeYmaera X proofs; the red-blood-cell, E. coli and Ariadne numbers come from runs in my working folder and are quoted, not reproduced, here. Kinetic parameters in the toy models are partly assumed. Exact generators reach the red-blood-cell model (20 after FVA reduction) but not the genome-scale E. coli pair, whose results come from LP certificates instead.
